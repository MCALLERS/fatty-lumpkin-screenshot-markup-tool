## Text

The short quotations use the 1922 Ogden/Ramsey English edition, reproduced by [The Ludwig Wittgenstein Project](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php/Tractatus_Logico-Philosophicus_(English)). That edition is identified there as public domain.

- **2.1:** “We make to ourselves pictures of facts.”
- **2.12:** “The picture is a model of reality.”
- **4.1212:** “What can be shown cannot be said.”

The last quotation concerns what logical form shows, rather than a general claim that photographs replace language. The early picture theory includes propositions as pictures of possible situations; it is not simply “images carry more information than words.”

The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wittgenstein/) verifies the Cambridge encounter with Bertrand Russell in 1911, the early picture theory, and the later emphasis on language in use and language-games. The German Tractatus appeared in 1921; its English edition appeared in 1922. Philosophical Investigations appeared posthumously in 1953. The later work criticizes the earlier general account of language; it does not make pictures useless. “He changed his mind. We’re keeping the pencil.” is our joke, not a quotation.
