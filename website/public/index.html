EARLY LUDWIG. A FEW GOOD PICTURES.

A philosopher.
A very opinionated pencil.

Wittgenstein took pictures seriously.
We thought that was a good place to start.

In the early Tractatus, a proposition can picture a possible situation through a shared structure. Our practical version: put an example in front of someone, then mark what matters.

  1. 1911

    Enter Bertrand Russell.

    Wittgenstein begins studying with Russell at Cambridge. Two minds with rather a lot to discuss.

  2. 1921–22

    The picture takes shape.

    The Tractatus appears in German in 1921, then in English in 1922, with an introduction by Russell. This is our early Ludwig.

  3. 1953

    He changed his mind.

    His later work questions a single picture theory of language, turning to meaning in use and language-games. Philosophical Investigations is published posthumously in 1953.

    We’re keeping the pencil.

A FEW SMALL DETAILS

Before you
make your mark.

What do I need?

An Apple silicon Mac running macOS 14 or later, plus an iPad on the same trusted network. Use the Safari companion without TestFlight, or install the optional native TestFlight beta on iPadOS 18+. Apple Pencil makes handwriting natural. The setup guide walks you through pairing and your screenshot folder.

Does Ludwig’s send anything to an AI?

No. You paste or attach the finished PNG to the AI conversation you choose. Ludwig’s does not train an AI, submit prompts, or guarantee a particular result.

Where do my pictures go?

By default, captures and finished images live under Documents/Screenshot Tool on your Mac, in Inbox and Ready. The app keeps annotated output separate from originals.

Is this really free?

Yes. The app is free and open source. Bring your own Mac, iPad, and AI tools.
